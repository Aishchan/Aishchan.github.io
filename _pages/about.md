Aishwarya Chandrasekaran is a remote sensing specialist with over a decade of experience (and an uncanny knack for making NDVI maps look cool), she blends cutting-edge tech with climate consciousness to educate the next generation of geospatial thinkers.

Passionate about continuous learning and dedicated to educating future scholars in remote sensing, GIS, and programming, Aishwarya believes staying current is essential in a world where technology and spatial data evolve at warp speed. As the planet becomes increasingly interconnected, she sees education not just as a classroom activity but as a lifelong pursuit—one where curiosity and code go hand in hand.

Currently a lecturer and aspiring research professor, she teaches courses in GeoAI, remote sensing fundamentals, spatial visualization, and forestry data acquisition—often spicing up classroom theory with real-world projects in collaboration with the Climate Adaptation Intern Program and Data Science & AI centers. Her new course, GeoAI Ethics and Society, proves that she doesn’t just train students to analyze pixels, but to also analyze power, bias, and the societal impact of AI.

She mentors students like a geospatial Yoda, guiding them through projects that explore everything from biking accessibility to tree species classification. Her research agenda reads like a techie’s dream: machine learning classifiers, hardwood species mapping, wildfire risk modeling, cloud computing, and all things geospatial.

When she’s not decoding forest canopies from 500 miles above, she’s probably crafting a seminar on digital tools for forestry, plotting how to replace ground plots with smarter AI, or dreaming up acronyms for her future lab (yes, she takes requests).

Part academic, part environmental problem-solver, part tech nerd—with a flair for turning dense remote sensing algorithms into stories students remember—Aishwarya is on a mission to map the world better, fairer, and a little more ethically.
